
# Change Management Plan

## Proposed Adoption of Analytics-Assisted Aircraft Recovery

**Project:** Delta Air Lines Flight Delay Analytics  
**Course:** BUSI6029 — Business Analytics Project 1

## 1. Purpose

This change management plan outlines how an airline
could progressively adopt a predictive–prescriptive
aircraft recovery decision-support system.

The proposed system combines machine-learning
delay forecasts with mathematical optimisation
to support aircraft recovery decisions.

The plan is conceptual. The system has not been
implemented by Delta Air Lines.

## 2. Strategic Nature of Change

The proposed change includes four dimensions.

### Data-Driven Decision Practices

Operational staff would receive analytical
recommendations alongside existing operational
information and professional judgement.

### Process and Workflow Redesign

Aircraft recovery decisions would include forecast
review, operational feasibility checks, human
approval, and post-decision monitoring.

### New Performance Monitoring

Additional KPIs could include:

- Forecast error during disruptions.
- Historically and operationally feasible
  recovery recommendations.
- Recommendation acceptance rates.
- Modelled turnaround-shortfall change.
- Decision response time.
- Operational reliability and stakeholder feedback.

### Technology Integration

The prototype would require more timely
aircraft-arrival information and integration
with relevant scheduling and dispatch systems.

## 3. Perception of Change

The proposed introduction is primarily adaptive
and transitional.

Adaptive change involves learning new analytical
practices and integrating them into existing work.

Transitional change involves moving from current
decision processes toward structured analytics-
assisted workflows.

A wider transformation could emerge through
long-term digital adoption, but that outcome has
not been established by this academic project.

## 4. Forces Initiating Change

### External Forces

- Competitive pressure to improve service reliability.
- Increasing availability of analytics technologies.
- Customer expectations for reliable operations.
- Safety and regulatory expectations.

### Internal Forces

- Need for timely disruption-response decisions.
- Potential inefficiencies in aircraft recovery planning.
- Opportunities for more consistent decision support.
- Interest in improving analytical capability.

These are proposed drivers of change rather than
verified findings about Delta's internal operations.

## 5. Forces Restraining Change

Potential restraining forces include:

- Employee uncertainty about algorithmic recommendations.
- Concern that automation could reduce decision autonomy.
- Limited availability of current operational data.
- Insufficient training and analytical capability.
- Integration costs and technical complexity.
- Existing routines and organisational incentives.
- Safety concerns when predictions are inaccurate.

The project provides direct evidence of forecast
reliability concerns.

In the retrospective forecast-assisted experiment,
only four of eight recommended swaps passed the
represented historical scheduling checks.

## 6. Change Agents

### Operations Sponsor

Provides strategic direction, resources, and
accountability for the proposed pilot.

### Airline Dispatch and Recovery Lead

Translates operational requirements into
decision rules and validates recovery workflows.

### Business Analytics Team

Develops forecasts, monitors model quality,
and evaluates optimisation recommendations.

### IT and Data Engineering Team

Supports data integration, system reliability,
and information access controls.

### Frontline Change Champions

Gather user feedback, support training,
and help colleagues adopt the new workflow.

## 7. Functions of Change Agents

### Sensemaking and Articulation

Explain the operational problem and the
purpose and limitations of analytics-based
decision support.

### Problem Diagnosis

Identify data-quality weaknesses, forecast
errors, scheduling limitations, and decision
bottlenecks.

### Stakeholder and Resistance Management

Listen to dispatch staff, involve operational
experts, and address safety and autonomy concerns.

### Implementation and Institutionalisation

Coordinate pilot testing, operating procedures,
training, model monitoring, and periodic review.

## 8. Planned Change Framework

### Phase 1 — Unfreeze

- Communicate why the proposed change is needed.
- Discuss potential benefits and limitations.
- Engage relevant operational stakeholders.
- Assess readiness and data availability.
- Define safety and approval requirements.

### Phase 2 — Change

- Introduce a limited decision-support pilot.
- Train dispatch and recovery personnel.
- Integrate updated aircraft-arrival information.
- Add aircraft compatibility and crew checks.
- Test recommendations before operational adoption.
- Collect feedback and monitor prediction errors.

### Phase 3 — Refreeze

- Formalise validated operating procedures.
- Establish model monitoring and review responsibilities.
- Maintain human override and escalation mechanisms.
- Regularly assess user satisfaction and effectiveness.
- Update decision rules when performance deteriorates.

## 9. Intervention Types

| Intervention | Proposed Action |
|--------------|-----------------|
| People | Training, coaching, communication, user feedback |
| Process and Technology | Forecast dashboards, integrated data, recovery workflow redesign |
| Structural | Clear roles for model operation, approval, and escalation |
| Strategic and Cultural | Encourage evidence-based decisions while preserving operational judgement |

## 10. Organisational Effectiveness

### Goal Achievement

Question:
Does the system improve the quality and feasibility
of recovery decisions?

Possible KPI:
Share of recommended exchanges passing all required
operational feasibility checks.

### Efficiency

Question:
Does the system reduce turnaround disruption
or unnecessary decision effort?

Possible KPIs:
Verified turnaround-shortfall improvement and
decision processing time.

### Adaptability

Question:
Can the organisation respond to changing flight
conditions and data quality?

Possible KPIs:
Forecast update frequency, recalculation time,
and performance under disruption scenarios.

### Stakeholder Satisfaction

Question:
Do dispatchers, operational managers, and
other affected staff find the system useful?

Possible KPIs:
User satisfaction, adoption rates, and
qualitative feedback.

## 11. Risk Management

The academic experiments found a readiness
forecast MAE of 32.58 minutes and a maximum
optimistic error of 248.82 minutes within
the selected case-study population.

These findings suggest that deterministic
optimisation based only on schedule-derived
predictions would not be sufficiently reliable
for unsupervised deployment.

Proposed safeguards include:

- Human approval for recommended changes.
- Updated operational observations.
- Explicit forecast uncertainty assessment.
- Aircraft-type and equipment compatibility checks.
- Crew, maintenance, and network feasibility checks.
- Audit logs and model-performance monitoring.
- A no-swap fallback when safe recommendations
  cannot be established.

## 12. Implementation Recommendation

The proposed system should begin as an offline
decision-support prototype.

Subsequent controlled evaluation should verify
data completeness, forecast reliability,
technical integration, and operational feasibility
before any limited pilot.

Broader deployment should depend on demonstrated
performance and stakeholder approval.

The study does not establish that the prototype
is ready for live airline operations.

## 13. Conclusion

The proposed organisational change is not merely
the introduction of a machine-learning model.

It requires coordinated adjustments to people,
processes, technology, decision authority,
and organisational culture.

A successful change programme must prioritise
operational safety, transparency, reliable
information, and continuous improvement.
